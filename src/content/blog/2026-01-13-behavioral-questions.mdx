## STAR methodology

S – Situation: A description of the circumstance leading up to the skill being demonstrated. Don’t give too many details, just enough so that the interviewer understands the scenario.
T – Task: What were you doing and what was the goal?
A – Action: What you did specifically to achieve that goal. There is no ‘we’ in this section, just ‘I’.
R – Result: What was the outcome as a result of your performance? Try to include figures and statistics if you have them.

## Most Commonly Asked Behavioral Questions

Q. What was the most difficult decision you had to make?

Situation

The product manager was changing countries in the middle of our alpha preview of Database monitoring. We had meetings lined up with 8 Splunk O11y Cloud customers and he couldn't be available for the first 1 month.

Task

I had to coordinate with the engineering manager, the documentation writer, and our product marketing manager on who will be driving these meetings.

Action

Since I was closest to product from an end-to-end perspective, I ended up taking charge of meetings for the first half of the alpha preview. I wrote a script on how these recurring meetings would be handled to ensure these customers felt heard and were able to see the full advantage of the new feature that we were in the process of releasing. I had help from engineering manager for tasks which required technical finesse specifically around configuring our telemetry pipelines in the backend.

Result

We had a successful 2 month alpha preview which consisted of weekly recurring meetings with 8 Splunk O11y Cloud customers spanning a total of close to 60 hours.

Q. What are your greatest strengths? Weaknesses?

My greatest strength is being obsessed with technical details. I believe good developer experiences can only be designed by being well versed with the quirks of the technology keeping in line with the philosophy of bauhaus principles.

My greatest weakness is sometimes getting too caught up in technical details, which can slow down decision-making. I'm actively working on balancing attention to detail with efficiency.

Q. What was the most significant thing you accomplished in your most recent job?

Launching the database monitoring extension on Splunk O11y Cloud. This happened right after the Splunk acquisition. I had to learn a new product, a new design system, coordinate with a new design team, as well as build relationships with a brand new workstream. On top of that, database monitoring was the first extension built on top of Splunk O11y Cloud after the acquistion. All prior extensions were Splunk's own acquisitions.

Q. Why should we hire you?

I'm deeply interested in the developer tooling space. I have both an engineering and design background which specifically targets this market. I also have prior experience working at both early stage startups as well as large enterprises, which gives me a unique perspective on how to build products fast as well for scale.

Q. What are your salary requirements?

I'm looking for base cash compensation of $260k annually with a target total compensation of $400k annually. However, I'm open to discussing the full compensation package including equity, bonuses, and benefits.

This also uses a noun-verb approach to define jobs which are then strung together to form workflows. This was presented to the entire design team last year and has seen a slow but favorable adoption.
